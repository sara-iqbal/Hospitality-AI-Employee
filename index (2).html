Five problems in one enquiry desk

Each row below is one specific way an enquiry can be lost between the moment it arrives and the moment a booking should have happened, with what changes when an AI employee handles it instead.

Slow first response
Enquiries received outside working hours can sit for hours. By the time someone replies, the guest has often already booked elsewhere.
Fixed
Every enquiry answered in about 18 seconds, at any hour, on every channel.
Inconsistent qualification
Reading a free-text message and correctly capturing dates, room type and party size takes attention that gets rushed on a busy day. Roughly a third of enquiries get something wrong the first time.
Fixed
Structured intent is extracted from every message the same way, every time, and flagged for a person only when something is genuinely unclear.
Next action never arranged
Even a well-qualified enquiry can stall if nobody actually locks in a hold, a quote or a viewing before the conversation goes quiet.
Fixed
A concrete next step is proposed and confirmed inside the same conversation, before the guest has to ask again.
Fragmented customer records
An enquiry that comes in by phone lives in someone's notebook, one from WhatsApp lives on a personal phone, and nothing connects them if the same guest gets in touch twice.
Fixed
Every enquiry, on every channel, lands in one record with the full conversation attached.
No visibility for leadership
Without a single record of what came in, nobody can see which channel is underperforming or how much revenue is quietly at risk.
Fixed
A live rollup by channel, with response times and revenue at risk visible without asking anyone to compile it.

02

03
Qualified
Dates, room type and party size extracted from the message.
04
Next action arranged
A hold, quote or viewing confirmed with the guest directly.
05
Handed to the team
Full context attached, only when a person is actually needed.
